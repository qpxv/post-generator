adaptability: the speed at which you can change your behavior in response to new conditions
adaptation: learning specifically in response to a mismatch between behavior and the demands of a situation
addiction: a compulsion toward something that persists despite its negative consequences
admiration: the involuntary recognition that someone has reached a standard you consider genuinely high
anger: a surge of negative energy  directed at someone/something that caused harm in a way you don't tolerate
belief: either certainty or faith depending on context
boredom: the failure to find something absorbing enough to occupy your spare attention
bravery: acting despite fear being present
calm: the absence of any situation where outcomes depend on immediate performance
certainty: to be sure about an outcome based on prior pattern-matching, with or without direct experience of that specific situation
compulsion: an urge to take an action that occurs without deliberate decision-making
confidence: acting as you would alone, adjusted only for genuine situational appropriateness, with the fear-of-judgment filter removed
connection: a relationship that provides genuine enjoyment of someone’s company, at varying levels of depth
connection (deep): being fully known in both your private and public life, accepted regardless, and supported practically, directionally, and emotionally as needed
connection (surface): genuine enjoyment of someone’s company without requiring depth
context: information that is relevant to interpreting, evaluating, or acting on other information
curiosity: the pull toward closing a specific gap between what you know and what you sense is knowable
data: a value or observation without the meaning supplied by a relevant relationship
discipline: the action that happens despite the absence of motivation to take said action
doubt: a signal that you need to change something, without telling you what
drive: the underlying reason that sustains movement toward a long term goal
ego: the impulse to protect your self-image from being diminished
empathy: either empathy (cognitive), empathy (emotional) or both
empathy (cognitive): accurately predicting what someone else is feeling or will feel, before they consciously register it themselves
empathy (emotional): involuntarily feeling what someone else is feeling alongside them
expectation: a standard you hold for someone else’s behavior, either communicated (reliability) or assumed (manners)
faith: to be sure about an outcome with no evidential base, direct or indirect
fear: the internal response to a perceived threat
fearlessness: the genuine absence of a fear response to a perceived threat
focus: the ability to direct and maintain attention on a single task
frustration: a surge of negative energy directed at the obstacle of a task that seems unsolvable in the current moment
guilt: failing to uphold the standard you set for yourself for an action
hard-working: the continuous application of discipline through boredom on a given task
hedonic adaptation: the gradual loss of novelty by repeated exposure
information: data with enough relationship/structure attached to it that it communicates something
intelligence: the speed and accuracy with which you extract and apply relevant patterns from information
judgment: being mentally ranked or evaluated by someone else in a way you cannot control
learning: extracting usable information from past experience that changes future behavior
loneliness: the absence of connection at the depth you require
manners: a set of behavior standards implicitly agreed upon by a social group for a given situation
motivation: the internal response that creates the desire to take an action
novelty: the capacity of a positive stimulus to still produce a reward response
opinion: a personal position on something that cannot be objectively verified
patience: tolerating delay without acting to force the expected outcome
pressure: an external situation where the outcome depends on your performance, with negative consequences for failure
regret: wishing a past outcome had been different
reliability: consistently doing what you communicated you would do, within the agreed upon timeframe
respect (basic): treating someone according to the standard you believe every person deserves by default, until their behavior crosses your tolerance threshold
respect (earned): certainty in someone’s ability combined with admiration for the standard you hold that they have met or exceeded
respect (self): certainty in your own ability combined with admiration for the standard you have demonstrated to yourself
sadness: the withdrawal of energy in response to a loss you cannot undo
standard: a self imposed bar for the minimally accepted quality of execution
structure: a defined order for how something should be done
support (directional): belief in someone’s path, pulling them forward
support (emotional): receiving someone’s feelings without judgment, giving them relief
support (practical): removing a burden from someone’s path
threat: an external situation or entity with the potential to cause harm
tolerance: the amount of [X] you can absorb before it forces a response
trust: either reliability (in a person), certainty (in yourself or a process), or support (directional) depending on context
uncertainty: the absence of knowable outcomes before an action is taken
